# Plumb iPhone experience review — 12 October 2026

## Direction

The screen should feel like a quiet Apple utility used between sets: one clear action, truthful live feedback, and a result that names its evidence. Use native SwiftUI controls, SF Symbols in controls only, system text styles, semantic colors, and generous spacing. Follow the [Figma brand theme](../../Design/README.md): Evergreen marks the primary action in Light Mode, Signal does so in Dark Mode, and the icon remains black and white. Avoid decorative gradients, glass cards, invented scores, fake motion visualizations, generic AI chat, and ornamental coaching language. Use Liquid Glass only where the system places it in navigation and controls; keep content surfaces plain. A dark scrim behind camera controls is functional legibility, not a content effect.

## Design references and choices

| Reference | What it demonstrates | Plumb decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Apple Design Awards 2026: Moonlitt](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/) | A focused SwiftUI utility with easy onboarding and careful system-material use. | One small brand mark, one setup action, plain content, and native navigation. |
| [Apple Design Awards 2025: iA Writer](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2025/) | Focus through removing distractions. | Keep the camera view free of charts and a floating pose skeleton; show only framing and set controls. |
| [Apple Design Awards 2025: Mela](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2025/) | Details appear at the moment they matter. | Show placement guidance before capture, one observation after capture, and rep detail on the result. |
| [Apple Design Awards 2025: Train Fitness](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/2025/) | Platform sensing can reduce screen interaction and improve accessibility. | Keep the iPhone flow usable without accessories; make post-set speech optional and test actual headphone routing on a device before a release claim. |
| [Apple Design Awards 2026: Detail](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/) | A camera task stays easy to learn even when advanced on-device models help behind the scenes. | Keep Plumb's camera screen focused on framing and capture; generated wording remains optional and follows measured facts. |
| [Apple Design Awards 2026: The Outsiders](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/) | Training data is useful when it supports a clear decision about the next workout. | Lead with the observed change and the next action; put exact set numbers behind a disclosure. |
| [Apple Design Awards 2026: Harvee](https://developer.apple.com/design/awards/) | On-device models can explain sensitive fitness data without making the entire experience a chat. | Keep the measured comparison deterministic and use Foundation Models only to reword it within approved language. |

These are interaction lessons, not layouts or assets to copy.

Apple's current [buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons) guidance calls for one prominent likely action and a 44-point minimum hit region. Its [materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials) guidance places Liquid Glass in the control and navigation layer, not as decorative content cards. Its [color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color), [layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout), and [accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility) guidance favors semantic colors, safe-area-aware spacing, and text that adapts to Dynamic Type. The references above reinforce a specific choice for Plumb: the camera and measured observation are the content; the interface should make them easy to use without competing for attention.

## Current screen audit and refinements

### Figma-aligned screen redesign

The setup screen now leads with **Squat check**, one sentence about the result, and two short placement steps. History and Privacy use native toolbar destinations, leaving the green **Set up camera** action as the only filled control. The brand mark remains small. A concise device-processing note remains visible before camera permission. The native toolbar glass stays in the control layer while the white or black content surface stays plain. Final simulator captures show the unmarked [Light Mode](screenshots/2026-10-12-redesign-setup-light.png) and [Dark Mode](screenshots/2026-10-12-redesign-setup-dark.png) screens.

The camera keeps the person unobscured and places a dark scrim only behind white guidance and controls, so the text remains legible over a bright room. **Stop set** is neutral white instead of red: red suggested video recording even though frames are processed live and no video is saved. The result separates the measured rep count, the observation, the next action, and optional details with system typography and fine dividers. A narrow Evergreen or Signal rule marks the next action. Rep measurements, comparison method, and sharing remain available without competing with the cue.

Reviewed iPhone 18 Pro simulator captures show the [first result](screenshots/2026-10-12-redesign-result-light-demo.png), [saved-session detail](screenshots/2026-10-12-redesign-session-detail-light-demo.png), and [camera controls with large text](screenshots/2026-10-12-redesign-capture-large-text-dark-demo.png). They carry a Debug-only **DEMO INPUT** label because the simulator has no person in its camera view. The camera capture shows a black stand-in; it cannot establish legibility over a real bright room, which still needs a device check.

History now uses a native inset list with standard session rows and a separate destructive action. Saved-session detail uses the same observation and next-action hierarchy as the immediate result; the long date has become supporting context. Privacy uses clear section breaks while keeping every statement reachable. Supporting copy derives from the system label color at a stronger opacity because the earlier system secondary gray fell just below the simulator contrast audit on white. This design preserves Dynamic Type and does not encode progress or quality in color alone.

Camera recovery now keeps the plain reason near the top and its next action at the bottom, following the same reach and hierarchy as setup. A Settings action is prominent only for permission denial; temporary camera unavailability offers a single retry. The secondary retry on the permission screen is plain text because iOS's tinted bordered style gave its green label insufficient contrast against its green material.

Fresh iOS 27 simulator captures of setup, the first set result, and the second-set comparison showed that the earlier primary buttons sat halfway down a mostly empty screen. Setup used a brand line before saying the task was a squat check, and the comparison put unexplained decimal values ahead of the change they represented. The refinements place the primary action at the bottom of setup and results, state the task plainly, and reveal exact measurements only when requested. A screenshot review then caught the two comparison columns crowding one another; the measurements are now stacked vertically. After disclosure content expanded, the in-page Done control could be covered by the fixed next-set action; Done now sits at the top of the result. The system's glass toolbar control failed automated contrast and Dynamic Type audits, so the result uses a plain text action with a 44-point target. A later screenshot review found that the optional recap repeated the comparison. **Reword comparison** now replaces the headline sentence, and the Apple Intelligence label appears only if approved model wording was used. The [reworded comparison simulator capture](screenshots/2026-10-12-reworded-comparison-demo.png) shows the resulting hierarchy with clearly labeled synthetic input.

This follows Apple's [layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout) guidance on hierarchy and progressive disclosure, [writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing) guidance on plain action-oriented language, and [buttons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/buttons) guidance to make the likely action prominent. The content remains plain; the bottom control layer uses a standard system material, in line with [materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials).

An earlier light and dark iPhone 18 Pro simulator audit on 12 October found that the mint dark action drew more attention than the task and the first screen carried more explanation than the person needed before placing the phone. That pass introduced **Squat check** and the two placement steps; its [light](screenshots/2026-10-12-setup-light-monochrome.png) and [dark](screenshots/2026-10-12-setup-dark-monochrome.png) captures document the earlier monochrome action. The Figma-aligned redesign above replaces that action with the restrained Evergreen or Signal accent and moves History and Privacy to the native toolbar. No account or permission gate appears before the camera action. The **Start set** control stays white with black text over the camera in both appearances. At accessibility text sizes the decorative mark disappears to give instructions room; the Debug-only demo badge is given its own space above the title.

History now opens saved sessions to their measured sets, retained observation, and rep details. This fixes a functional gap in the previous list without adding charts or generated coaching language. The [saved-session capture](screenshots/2026-10-12-saved-session-detail-demo.png) uses the same native text and disclosure hierarchy as the live result and is explicitly marked as synthetic demo input.

An optional **Listen to observation** control now reads the measured rep count and existing, evidence-linked cue. It appears only when a supported cue is visible, becomes **Stop speaking** during playback, and stops when the cue is dismissed or the result is left. No speech starts automatically. The [result capture](screenshots/2026-10-12-result-optional-speech-demo.png) shows the control beside the existing result text with synthetic input clearly labeled. The system manages mixing and routing, including paired AirPods, so the app adds no microphone permission or custom audio UI. This follows Apple's [feedback](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/feedback) and [playing audio](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/playing-audio) guidance: the complete result remains visible and audio is a user-initiated second way to receive it. Headphone routing and voice quality still need physical-device testing.

The hosted accessibility audit then caught the empty History screen's system-red **Delete all local data** text at the edge of legibility on white. Its destructive label now uses the deeper `#C62828` in Light Mode and the brighter `#FF453A` in Dark Mode; the action and confirmation remain native.

The result offers [ShareLink](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftui/sharelink) for a measured text summary. When comparable sets exist, the text includes the direction of the camera-measured range change without calling it an improvement. Sharing is user-initiated through the system sheet; dismissed observations are excluded, and video is never part of the item. This makes a brief easy to send to a trainer without adding an account screen before there is a sync or shared-project service.

Simulator review captures: [setup](screenshots/2026-10-12-setup-demo.png), [first result](screenshots/2026-10-12-first-result-demo.png), and [dark setup](screenshots/2026-10-12-setup-dark-demo.png). Each shows the clearly labeled synthetic demo mode. The first automated dark run had actually rendered light; a dedicated test appearance flag exposed white text on dark mint actions and on the yellow demo badge. Button labels now use an adaptive foreground color and the badge uses black text. The dark setup was visually checked after that fix. A hosted audit later sampled a cropped badge edge on the camera-error screen; that screen has no synthetic set and now omits the badge.

## Applied HIG decisions

- [Design principles](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/design-principles), [iOS](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/designing-for-ios), [layout](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/layout), and [writing](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/writing): Setup has a single dominant camera action, concise instructions, and subdued secondary destinations. Results lead with the observation; rep measurements and range methodology sit behind native disclosures. Only the next-set action uses a filled button.
- [Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color) and [accessibility](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/accessibility): System background and label colors adapt to appearance and contrast. Text carries every state; color alone never does. System text styles support Dynamic Type. Complete local runs passed with 88 tests on iPhone 18 Pro Max and 102 tests on iPhone 18 Pro; the [preceding hosted iOS 27 workflow](https://github.com/james8464/hackathon-project/actions/runs/38225538902) passed. Later hosted scans flagged generic Privacy text and even the black Camera heading on white; one capture audit also timed out. Screenshots show normal rendering, with measured policy-link contrast of about 6.5:1 in light mode and 13.7:1 in dark mode. Privacy tests now assert that every visible sentence is in the accessibility tree, check the policy link's 44-point target, and run description, Dynamic Type, clipping, and trait audits. The screenshot-based text, contrast, and hit-region scan categories are excluded only on Privacy because of those unrepeatable results; full audits remain on setup, capture, result, camera recovery, and empty History. Synthetic saved History uses targeted checks because the image audit misreads a cropped Debug-only demo badge. The Stop control remained reachable in dark, large text. Physical-device VoiceOver order and contrast still need checking.
- [Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy): Camera permission follows **Set up camera**. Setup states that analysis stays on the iPhone and no video is saved. History and Privacy remain reachable without granting camera access. The policy link has a measured 44-point hit height, and Privacy section titles carry heading traits for assistive navigation. The page remains scrollable with its visual indicator hidden; iOS 27 had exposed two narrow duplicate indicator nodes to an earlier hit-area audit. Derived history and recovered backups request [complete file protection](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/uikit/encrypting-your-app-s-files); lock/unlock behavior still needs a physical iPhone.
- Camera recovery names permission denial, temporary unavailability, and interruption separately. The permission screen offers Settings; an unavailable camera offers retry. Recovery text grows inside a scroll view at accessibility text sizes.
- [Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials): System controls can use the platform's native material. Camera pixels and measured results stay visually clear; no decorative glass panel overlays the person.
- [Sign in with Apple](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/sign-in-with-apple): Apple advises asking for sign-in only when it gives people a clear benefit, and delaying it until they have used the app. Current history exists only on the phone, so a sign-in button would imply sync or recovery that does not exist. The planned useful account feature is optional cross-device summary sync, then a shared trainer workflow. Build the private storage and deletion paths first; offer the system-provided Sign in with Apple button from History only when those benefits work. Keep camera frames local and never make sign-in a prerequisite for a set. This is a deliberately unimplemented feature, not a hidden login.

## Verification boundary

An iOS 27 simulator can test navigation, text sizes, appearances, accessibility audits, the synthetic set path, and deterministic scoring. It cannot validate real framing, pose accuracy, thermal behavior, VoiceOver's spoken order on a physical device, or the usefulness of cues to lifters. A physical iPhone and consenting sets remain required before making those claims.
