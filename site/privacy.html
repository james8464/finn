Privacy policy

Plumb reviews supported side-view exercises from your iPhone camera without saving a workout video or requiring an account.

Effective date: 13 October 2026 · Publisher: James Durup

Camera and workout data

With your permission, Plumb uses the camera during a set to estimate body position and count camera-visible repetitions. Camera frames are processed on your iPhone. Plumb does not record a movie, save camera frames, or upload them to a Plumb server. The temporary pose trace is held in memory for the current result and cleared when you finish the session or start a new capture.

Plumb saves derived set summaries on your iPhone: session and set identifiers, date, exercise and rubric version, camera side, rep timing, relative range, optional per-rep posture readings, analysis confidence, a selected observation if any, your response to that observation, and, if you save workouts to Apple Health, when the session was saved and Health's identifier for that workout. These summaries are stored in the app's local Application Support area. They are not automatically sent to us or shared with other users. If you tap Share summary after a measured set, the iOS Share Sheet lets you choose where to send a text summary of the rep count and any observation you have not marked irrelevant. When Plumb can compare two compatible sets, the text also states the direction of the camera-measured range change. It contains no video or camera frames.

Backups and deletion

iOS may include local Application Support data in your device backup, including iCloud Backup if you enable it. Plumb does not operate or access that backup.

You can open History to delete a saved session or choose Delete all local data. The latter also removes a damaged-history backup created if you explicitly recover corrupted local history. These actions remove data from the app on the current device; they do not erase an earlier iPhone backup. Removing the app also removes its local app data under normal iOS behavior.

Optional Apple Intelligence wording

When available on your device, Plumb can ask Apple's on-device Foundation Models framework to reword an already measured comparison after you tap Reword comparison. It receives only the two derived set measurements, comparison direction, cue category, and referenced rep numbers. Plumb checks the response and shows reviewed fallback wording if generation is unavailable, late, or inconsistent. This feature does not send camera images or raw pose traces to the model, and Plumb does not operate an AI server.

Spoken reps and observations

Unless you turn off Say reps aloud in Options, iOS speaks the countdown, each counted rep, a short live observation when a rep is clearly faster or shorter than your first reps, the set's outcome, and, when hands-free framing cannot see what it needs, which joint is unclear. After you tap Listen to observation, iOS speaks the measured rep count, displayed observation, and next action. Speech goes through the current audio output, such as AirPods. Plumb does not record or save this speech or use the microphone. Playback of an observation stops when you leave the result, mark the observation irrelevant, or tap Stop speaking.

Hands-free sets and your most frequent pattern

With hands-free on (the default, changeable in Options), the app starts a set once you hold still in position and ends it when you walk away or stop moving, using the same on-device pose analysis. Each saved set summary also lists which measured patterns the set showed, such as movements too shallow to count, so the app can show your most frequent one before the next set. These are derived facts in the same local summaries, deleted with them.

Apple Health and the Fitness app (optional)

Save workouts to Apple Fitness in Options is off until you turn it on. When you turn it on, iOS asks whether Plumb may save workouts to Apple Health; Plumb asks for nothing else and reads no Health data. Once you allow it, each time you tap Done after a session with at least one measured set, Plumb writes one workout to Health on your iPhone: functional strength training, indoor, its start and end time (from the first measured rep to the end of the last set, with breaks over five minutes recorded as pauses), and three Plumb values: the number of measured sets, the measured reps, and the exercise names. Plumb never writes calories, heart rate, video, pose points, or observations to Health. Plumb remembers in its local summary that the session was saved, so it does not save it twice.

Health then holds the workout under your Health settings and Apple's privacy policy, not Plumb's: it appears in Health and the Fitness app, iOS may count it toward your Activity rings, and Health may sync it to your other devices through iCloud if you use that. Nothing leaves your iPhone through Plumb, and we cannot see these workouts. Deleting history in Plumb does not remove workouts already saved to Health; delete them in the Health app. You can stop future saves by turning the option off, and withdraw Plumb's permission in Health settings at any time. Data from demo or recorded input is never saved to Health. Plumb does not use Health data for advertising or share it with anyone.

Accounts, analytics, and sharing

The current release candidate has no account, advertising SDK, third-party analytics, cloud sync, or in-app purchase flow. Its only HealthKit use is the optional workout saving above, which reads no Health data. It does not ask for microphone access. Plumb does not record which Share Sheet destination you choose or upload the summary to a Plumb server. If these practices change, this policy and the App Store privacy answers will be updated before release.

Your choices and contact

You can deny camera access or change it in iOS Settings. Without camera access, live set review is unavailable. You can delete local results in the app. For questions about this policy or data handling, contact jamesdurup@icloud.com.

This policy covers the app's current implementation. Separately consented research recordings used to validate the product follow a separate process.
